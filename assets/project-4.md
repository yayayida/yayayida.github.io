# Mobility Patterns Analysis in Lisboa
**Sep 2024 – Nov 2024 · Lisbon, Portugal**

Question: How to encourage first- and last-mile modal choice in Lisboa?
- Inhomogeneous spatial distribution of Micromobility (Gira-Shared bike in Lisboa; E-scooters): Multimodal Accessibility Analysis
- Bikes: Cycling Transition Modal-shift matrix: 1,272 survey responses about the willingness to use bikes from IST’s community; decision tree; reduced emissions estimation; Decision-making in Bike selection: Multi-Attribute Utility Theory (MAUT) Model and Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) Model
- E-scooters: Social-demographic influencing factors; Decision-making in e-scooter operators (bird, uber, bolt, lime) based on survey

## Figures
There are various degrees of overlappings in commuting trips and recreational trips in both of routes and destinations. The main overlapping for routes are mainly concentrated in central Lisboa and Oriente area. Destinations for commuting purposes are mainly centred along the Avenues
that connect different plazas and squares with many office buildings and commercial centers. Interestingly it's the same route for Metro Amarela line and the main axis for the cycling network , which indicated the e-
scooters might be the favored last mile
options for office workers.
Main destinations for recreational
trips are heavily clustered along the
seaside Av. Brasilia from centred area
Cais do Sodre to Belem Tower till the
west coast. It makes sense as many
tourist spots on this avenue. There are
also significantly centralised
distribution of recreational
destinations in Baixa area and
extending along the Av. da Liberdade
till Marques de Pombal.

The two maps leftside illustrate the heatmap of
overall spatial distribution of origin-destination of
shared e-scooter trips in Lisboa.
By comparing these two maps, it can be seen that the
gradient distribution patterns for the most dense
grids are similar. Most dense girds with more origins
and destinations are concentrated along the seaside
avenues and the comercial buisness centres at all
times without consideration of traveling purposes.
This indicates the accessibility to e-scooters in these
areas is good, which means more service spots of e-
scooters in these area. There are also lots of grids in
bright yellow meaning very few trips in these areas.
The gradient heatmap of inhomogeneous spatial
distribution of e-scooter trips reflects the spatial
inequilities in accessing the micromobility vehicles.
By looking at the rightside maps demonstrating
destinations with different purposes, it has a close
match to the Lisboa cycling network. While for those
bright yellow areas on the left, the cycling network
are sparse and trips are few. This implied the
development of service infrastructures of e-scooters
and the development for comprehensive cycling
network should go in parallel to eliminate spatial
inequalities.
