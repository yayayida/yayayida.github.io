# Explainable AI for Maritime Autonomous Surface Ships (MASS): Adaptive Interfaces and Trustworthy Human-AI Collaboration
**Zhuoyue Zhang, Haitong Xu, Carlos Guedes Soares**

**Oct 2024 – Aug 2025 · CENTEC Lab · Lisbon, Portugal**

**Accepted, IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems, August 2026.**

This article provides a systematic review of automation transparency for Maritime Autonomous Surface Ships (MASS) across situation awareness (SA), human factors, interface design, and regulation. The review maps the Guidance, Navigation, and Control (GNC) stack to shore-based operational modes (remote supervision and remote control) and identifies where human unsafe control actions (Human-UCAs) concentrate in handover and emergency loops; summarizes evidence that transparency features such as decision rationales, alternatives, confidence or uncertainty, and rule-compliance indicators improve understanding and support trust calibration, while reliability and predictability often dominate overall trust; and distills design strategies for transparency at three layers: sensor and SA acquisition and fusion, HMI/eHMI presentation (textual or graphical overlays, color coding, conversational and immersive UIs), and engineer-facing processes (resilient interaction design, validation, and standardization). The review integrates methods for Human-UCA identification (STPA-Cog with IDAC), quantitative assessment of trust and SA, and operator workload monitoring, and outlines regulatory and rule-based implications, including COLREGs formalization and route exchange. It concludes with an adaptive transparency framework that couples operator state estimation with explainable decision support to reduce cognitive overload and improve takeover timeliness. The analysis highlights actionable figure-of-merit displays such as CPA/TCPA risk bars and robustness heatmaps, transparent model outputs including rule traceability and confidence, and training pipelines using HIL/MIL and simulation as near-term levers for safer MASS operations.
